The signing screen shows one document, start to finish, with nothing else on it.
There are two ways to reach it and they run the same process.

## Two ways in, one process

**Self-serve.** The person opens the document and signs it themselves.

**Manager-attested.** A manager signs off on behalf of a new starter who cannot
receive a login of their own — no personal device, no email — or who is not
comfortable enough with the technology to do it themselves. You open the same
document with that staff member selected, and the record is captured against
them, not you.

Manager attestation exists for those situations. It is not a shortcut for anyone
else.

The finished record is identical either way. What differs is who was driving.

## Initials, then the signature

A signature document asks for **initials on each page** and then one signature at
the end. The record captures each page separately, which is what the per-page
initials are for.

Work down the pages in order. The final signature only becomes available once the
initials above it are done.

## What is pinned, and why re-signing is sometimes right

A signature is pinned to **the version that was signed**. If the document is
updated afterwards, the old record stays true to what the person actually read —
it is not retroactively attached to the new text.

That is why a new version can put a document back on someone's outstanding list.
Nothing has gone wrong: the thing they agreed to has changed, so the agreement is
asked for again.

## If the screen says signing is unavailable

A document whose fields have not been confirmed cannot start a signing ceremony.
This is a deliberate stop, not a fault — it prevents someone initialling a
document whose fields were never checked. An admin confirms the fields on the
document's own screen, and signing opens up.
